# CA reference: Part I — Introduction

PDF pages 25-36, chapters 1-2. Framing, not procedure. No `ca-*` skill is
scoped to these chapters; they supply the motive the other cards assume.

## Ch.1 — What Is Design and Architecture? [CA ch.1 pp.25-32]

- There is no difference between design and architecture; the word architecture is used for the high level divorced from lower-level details, and design for structures and decisions at a lower level [CA ch.1 p.25]
- Low-level details and high-level structure are one continuous fabric, a continuum of decisions, with no clear dividing line [CA ch.1 p.26]
- The goal of software architecture is to minimize the human resources required to build and maintain the required system [CA ch.1 p.26]
- The measure of design quality is the measure of the effort required to meet the needs of the customer; effort that grows with each release means the design is bad [CA ch.1 p.26]
- The signature of a mess: with each release productivity declined, and the cost per line of code changed until the code was 40 times more expensive [CA ch.1 p.28]
- The developers' effort is diverted away from features and consumed with managing the mess [CA ch.1 p.29]
- Making messes is always slower than staying clean, on any time scale; the only way to go fast is to go well [CA ch.1 p.31]
- Starting over from scratch drives the redesign into the same mess, because the same overconfidence is doing the driving [CA ch.1 p.31]

## Ch.2 — A Tale of Two Values [CA ch.2 pp.33-36]

- Every software system provides two values to the stakeholders: behavior and structure [CA ch.2 p.33]
- Behavior is making the machine satisfy the requirements and fixing the bugs; programmers who believe that is the entirety of their job are mistaken [CA ch.2 p.33]
- Software was invented to be soft: the difficulty of a change should be proportional to the scope of the change, not to its shape [CA ch.2 p.34]
- A program that works perfectly and is impossible to change becomes useless when the requirements change [CA ch.2 p.34]
- A program that does not work and is easy to change can be made to work and kept working, so it remains continually useful [CA ch.2 p.34]
- Eisenhower's matrix: behavior is urgent but not always important, architecture is important but never particularly urgent [CA ch.2 p.35]
- The mistake is elevating features that are urgent and unimportant into first position, which sacrifices the architecture [CA ch.2 p.36]
- Asserting the importance of architecture over the urgency of features is the responsibility of the development team [CA ch.2 p.36]
- If architecture comes last, change eventually becomes practically impossible for part or all of the system [CA ch.2 p.36]
